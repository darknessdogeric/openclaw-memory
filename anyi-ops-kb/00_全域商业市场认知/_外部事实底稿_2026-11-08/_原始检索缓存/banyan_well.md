Banyan Group is entering 2026 after reaching its landmark 100-resort milestone, outlining a year focused on purposeful expansion and regenerative travel. The company says it now operates 100 hotels and resorts, more than 140 spas and galleries, and 20-plus branded residences across over 20 countries. The 2026 roadmap combines new market entries, a strengthened multi-brand pipeline, and a deeper push into wellbeing-led experiences. Eddy See has positioned the next phase as design-led growth anchored in responsible stewardship and long-term value for destinations and communities.

 A core theme for 2026 is geographic expansion into new and meaningful markets. In Africa, Banyan Group plans to enter safari hospitality with the grand opening of Ubuyu, a Banyan Tree Escape in Tanzania, marking its debut in East Africa, while Dhawa Ouidah is scheduled to open in Benin to extend the group’s presence into West Africa. In the Caribbean, the company will establish a foothold in the Dominican Republic through Angsana Cap Cana and Cassia Punta Cana, pairing a resort proposition with an extended-stay format. In Europe, Mamula Island by Banyan Tree is set to reopen for the 2026 season with design enhancements, refreshed dining concepts, signature rituals, and integrated spa and wellness programming intended to deliver a fuller Banyan Tree experience.

Across Asia and the Middle East, the group is advancing its multi-brand strategy with new openings across luxury, lifestyle, and urban brands. The Banyan Tree portfolio in China is set to grow with Banyan Tree Guangzhou Jiulong Lake, described as a modern ecological resort with Lingnan-inspired design, and Banyan Tree Mount Emei, located within a UNESCO World Heritage Site in Sichuan Province. The Angsana brand is expected to expand through Angsana Ashar Valley Tents in Saudi Arabia and Angsana Bogor in Indonesia. Additional pipeline highlights include Garrya Danang in Vietnam, further growth for Homm with Homm Mandaue Cebu, Homm Yibin Lizhuang, Homm Pondok Indah Jakarta, and Homm Palembang, and the launch of the group’s second Folio property with Folio Bogor in Indonesia.

Wellbeing is being scaled through the flagship brand experience, anchored by Banyan Tree Connections, a private holistic wellbeing journey for two designed around movement, mindfulness, and shared rituals in nature. After an initial rollout at Banyan Tree Anji, Banyan Tree Vabbinfaru, Banyan Tree Mayakoba, and Banyan Tree Phuket, the company says the programme will expand from April 2026 to eight additional resorts: Banyan Tree Nanjing Garden Expo, Banyan Tree Guangzhou Jiulong Lake, Banyan Tree Bintan, Buahan, a Banyan Tree Escape, Banyan Tree AlUla, Banyan Tree Samui, Banyan Tree Dubai, and Banyan Tree Lang Co.

The strategy is framed as a continuation of a milestone-heavy 2025. The group’s 100th resort, Mandai Rainforest Resort by Banyan Tree in Singapore, was marked by the inaugural Rainforest Festival, which attracted more than 4,000 visitors and raised over SGD 600,000 for the President’s Challenge in support of 52 charities and social service organisations through multiple funding streams. The company also highlighted anniversaries including 10 years of Cassia since Cassia Phuket, 20 years in China following Banyan Tree Ringha, and 30 years in both the Maldives and Indonesia connected to Banyan Tree Vabbinfaru and Banyan Tree Bintan.

Beyond hotels, Banyan’s 2026 narrative leans heavily on ecosystem growth. In 2025, the group expanded its branded residences portfolio with eight new sales launches, including Banyan Tree Padilla Madrid Residences in Madrid’s Salamanca district and the debut of its Bellaguna residential brand with Bellaguna Lake Residences - Lotus. It also highlighted ongoing stewardship initiatives, citing more than 30 Greater Good Grants across biodiversity, community wellbeing, education, culture, sustainable agriculture, and circular-economy themes. On the brand-extension side, Banyan Gallery continues to scale globally with long-running artisan partnerships, while the Banyan Spa & Wellbeing Academy remains a pipeline for talent development, having trained more than 2,800 therapists since 2001.
